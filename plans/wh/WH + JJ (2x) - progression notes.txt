WH + JJ (2x) — progression notes
================================

Companion to the WH + JJ (2x) - <Day>.fnw plans (a .fnw has no notes field, so
the cues that don't fit as reps/weight live here). Generated by
scripts/programs/wh_jj_2x.py.

This supersedes the older "WH + JJ" plan, which assumed jujitsu ~5x/week and used
all five weekdays as short post-JJ lifts. Jujitsu is now Tuesday + Thursday only.

Schedule
--------
Lifting Monday-Friday; JJ on Tue + Thu. (↔ = superset.)

  Mon  RDL ↔ stretch   /   Leg press ↔ curl ↔ split squat   /   Tib ↔ calf ↔ hyper ↔ ext rotation                  (full)
  Tue  Hip adduction ↔ abduction ↔ wrist rotation ↔ wrist extension   /   Neck: front ↔ back ↔ sides               (short, after JJ)
  Wed  Leg press ↔ curl ↔ stretch   /   Split squat ↔ tib ↔ calf ↔ hyper ↔ ext rotation                             (full)
  Thu  Hip adduction ↔ abduction ↔ wrist rotation ↔ wrist extension   /   Neck: front ↔ back ↔ sides               (short, after JJ)
  Fri  RDL ↔ stretch   /   Leg press ↔ curl ↔ split squat   /   Tib ↔ calf ↔ hyper ↔ ext rotation   /   Elephant walk  (full)

Mon/Wed/Fri are the full lower-body / posterior-chain / back-rehab days (not
after JJ, so a fresh back and time to warm up). Tue/Thu are SHORT: one dense hip
adduction/abduction machine circuit right after the class — you shower at the gym
anyway. Nothing else on those days.

The leg superset runs 3 rounds (leg press / curl / split squat); the hyper block
runs 3 rounds; the hip circuit runs 6 rounds. Wednesday is arranged differently
(stretch in SS1, split squat moved to SS2) — see the movement notes.

Low back is trained Mon/Wed/Fri only — never on consecutive days (Tue/Thu carry
no low-back work). The heavy snatch-grip RDL lands on Monday and Friday, the two
freshest-back days. No RDL on Wednesday means the back also rests before Thursday
JJ. Within a full day the two low-back hits are spread apart — RDL block first,
then the leg superset, then the hyper block — so the low back gets the most
recovery possible between them.

WH machines you don't have at home get the emphasis: the leg press (loaded for
glutes), the hip adduction/abduction machine, and the tibialis ("tip") machine.

Volume (why the set counts are what they are)
---------------------------------------------
A muscle needs >=12 sets/week (secondary muscles at 0.5, pooled across every
movement) to be progressed (add load OR reps). Below 12 is fine for maintenance.
Actual weekly totals from this plan:

  Progressing (>=12/wk): hamstrings 27, glutes 21.5, quads 15.5, low back 13,
    tibialis 12, adductors 12, abductors 12.
  Maintenance: calves 26 (12 direct + leg-press/curl secondaries), forearms 28
    (wrist prehab 12 each + RDL grip), rotator cuff 6 — kept light on purpose.

The full days (Mon/Wed/Fri) carry the entire low-back and glute/hamstring load,
so Tue/Thu can stay short without dropping any muscle below its floor.

Movement notes
--------------
- Leg press is for GLUTES: feet high, deep stretch. Standing in for squats until
  the back is strong enough to squat again. Load it up — no leg press at home.
- Hamstring curl: single-leg machine, reps per side, starting 12/side @ 100.
  Logged as "Hamstring Curl" (same as last year, history carries over).
- Split squat: quad/knee health, two big DBs at the sides (less low-back demand
  than a barbell front rack). Reps per side; weight = total of both DBs (two 45s
  = 90). WORKING sets by day: 3 on Mon/Fri, 2 on Wednesday = 8/wk. On Mon/Fri the
  split squat sits in the leg superset and you arrive warm (RDL block + the
  split-squat warm-ups done during the RDL rests). On WEDNESDAY it moves to the
  SECOND superset: with no RDL block to stretch during, Wednesday's SS1 is leg
  press + curl + ~8 min of couch stretch, and the split squats come after that in
  SS2 so they're properly warm (cold split squats in SS1 were poor). The bodyweight
  + light (two 25s) on-ramp is logged as WARM-UP sets (they don't count as volume).
- Leg press: 400 working, quick warm-up ramp (180 -> 280 -> 360, ~1 min), logged
  as warm-up sets so you're not hitting the working sets cold. On Wednesday the
  ramp is the LEG warm-up; the couch stretch in SS1 is the separate HIP warm-up
  for the split squats. Wednesday needs no hinge/back warm-up — you're not hinging
  except the very light hypers.
- RDL: snatch-grip, NO straps (grip trained raw). Held steady at 155 while the
  hypers progress. Warm-up ramp: 2x empty bar x12, 95x12, 135x8 — logged as
  WARM-UP sets, so they add gym time but not working volume. Mon + Fri only.
- Hyper + calf + tibialis + external rotation are one block (their machines, and
  the cables, are all next to each other at WH). The calf/tib/cuff work fills the
  rest between hyper sets — non-interfering (low back vs lower leg vs shoulder).
  This keeps it all OFF the RDL platform, so you never leave the platform
  unattended to cross the gym.
- The hyper block OPENS with tib + calf, then hyper, then external rotation. Tib
  and calf lead so they warm up the lower leg and each hyper set gets an ankle
  pair right before it as its rest. Calf and tib are EQUAL (4 each) so they pair
  up cleanly; because they're 4 and the hypers are 3, exactly one tib/calf pair
  lands at the very end (one trailing pair, instead of the two you'd get with the
  hyper listed first). Tib is the reason it's 4 (its 12/wk progression floor: 4 x
  3 days); calf rides along at the same count. Both land 12/wk direct (calf also
  gets the big leg-press/curl secondary pool on top).
- Stretches ride the RDL rest, done AT the platform (so it's never abandoned) and
  warming up the quads/hip flexors for the split squats. Couch stretch, 2 min/side,
  3 sets — a stretch in three of the four RDL rests. Add a hip stretch here if you
  want more; anything you can do standing at the platform works.
- Hip abduction is MAXED at the machine's 140 ceiling — progress by reps only.
  Adduction can still take load. 6 rounds each on Tue/Thu = 12/wk each. Current
  weights: abduction 140, adduction 90 (still ramping back up).
- Wrist prehab (rotation + extension): fills the rest during the 6 ad/ab rounds
  on Tue/Thu — the machines sit next to each other, so it's free rest-work. 6
  each = 12/wk. Anti-flexion (extensors, top of the forearm) + rotation balance
  the heavy JJ gripping and the raw-grip RDLs, keeping wrists and elbows healthy.
- Rotator cuff (cable external rotation): rides the hyper block on the cables next
  to the hyper machine, 2 sets x 3 full days = 6/wk, shoulder-health maintenance
  for all the grappling pulls/posts. It trains the external rotators — antagonists
  to the internal-rotation gripping/pulling in JJ. Light and higher-rep on purpose
  (never heavy; that's how a cuff gets tweaked). Kept OFF the short days so the
  tight T/Th circuit stays put at the ad/ab machines.
- Elephant walk: single set, Friday finisher — posterior-chain decompression.

Neck (machine, front / back / sides) — grappling-durability prehab
------------------------------------------------------------------
Done LAST on Tue/Thu. Starting 20 reps @ 30 lb each direction, 2 sets = 6 sets/day
(12/wk). Front = flexion, back = extension, sides = lateral flexion.

- Sides: log one set = BOTH sides (per-side counts once, the repo convention).
  Neck isn't a floor-tracked muscle, so it doesn't actually matter — log whatever
  reads easiest.
- Progression is double progression: ramp reps to ~35, then take the SMALLEST
  weight bump and reset reps. Ramp reps FAST (you're far from failure, not sore);
  ramp weight PATIENTLY — the neck's connective tissue and small stabilizers lag
  the muscle, and a tweak flares a day or two later and costs weeks. Cap weight
  jumps to ~once every 1-2 weeks for the first couple months.
- Watch delayed soreness, especially since you roll T/Th. If a neck session
  leaves you tender for JJ or sleep, hold the load where it is.
- Form: controlled (2-3 sec), full but pain-free ROM, don't force loaded
  end-range (especially extension).

Still on the table: real upper-body strength (rows, presses) now that JJ isn't
supplying it 5x/week — a bigger add worth its own conversation if you want it.

Hyperextension progression (the rehab centerpiece)
--------------------------------------------------
9 sets/week (Mon/Wed/Fri, 3 each). With the RDL held steady, the hypers are the
low-back progression driver; 9 hyper sets plus the RDL's erectors put the low
back at ~13 (the tool counts the RDL's erectors at half).

The ladder (from Back_extension_progression.txt):
    1. 2 min hold
    2. 1 min hold each side, one leg
    3. 35 reps
    4. 20 reps each side, one leg
    5. Loaded reps (one or two legs)

Current status:
- Up to 5 reps one-legged hyperextension, and 5 reps curved-back
  hyperextension. These lead the FIRST 5 reps of the first two sets each
  session (then finish the set as regular reps toward 35).
- The one-legged STATIC HOLD (rung 2) was only ever completed on the 45-degree
  machine, never on the flat. WH has flat hyperextension machines, so restart the
  one-legged static hold on the FLAT machine this stint and work it up.

Weights in the .fnw are starting targets — adjust in FitNotes as you go.
