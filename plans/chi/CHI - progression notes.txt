CHI — progression notes
=======================

Companion to the CHI - <Day>.fnw plans (a .fnw has no notes field, so the cues
that don't fit as reps/weight live here). Generated by scripts/programs/chi_block.py.

This is the FIRST plan written for the Chicago home gym. Everything else under
plans/ is a travel gym: plans/wh/ is the machine gym, and plans/pit/ (with its
duplicate plans/pa/) plus plans/back_rehab/ and plans/back_rehab_jj/ are all
Pittsburgh.

Context
-------
Lifting Monday–Friday, up to ~90 min a session, no weekends. NO jujitsu for the
first month back. That makes month one the best lifting window of the block: no
grappling soreness, no recovery tax, and no delayed-soreness gate on the neck.

The two constraints that shape the week
---------------------------------------
Both point the same way, which is why the week splits into three big days and
two light ones.

1. GRIP is a long-standing limiter. Heavy grip on back-to-back days does not
   recover. So every grip-heavy movement is CLUSTERED onto the same days: the
   snatch-grip RDL (trained raw, no straps), the pulldown, the low row, and any
   loaded carry — including the calf raise, which is a 40 lb kettlebell held in
   one hand for the length of a set. The light days carry NO grip work at all;
   ring dips and L-sits are there because they are support grip, not the crush
   grip everything above taxes.

2. The POSTERIOR CHAIN is the same story. RDLs, hamstring work and the hypers
   cannot be spread across alternating days and recovered. So they cluster too,
   and the front rack split squat joins them — it taxes the hamstrings hard even
   though it costs no grip.

Wednesday runs the SAME SHAPE as Monday and Friday at reduced load — lighter
RDL, the paused split squat — rather than dropping movements. Alternating hinge
days with non-hinge days is the pattern that doesn't recover; a lighter day of
the same shape does.

Schedule
--------
(↔ = superset.)

  Mon  RDL ↔ stretch  /  Split squat ↔ incline ↔ tib  /  Hyper ↔ HSPU ↔ calf  /  Nordic ↔ pulldown
  Tue  Side hyper ↔ ring dip  /  Abduction ↔ QL raise ↔ L-sit  /  Neck
  Wed  RDL (light) ↔ stretch  /  Paused split squat ↔ incline ↔ tib  /  Hyper ↔ HSPU ↔ calf  /  Nordic ↔ low row
  Thu  Side hyper ↔ ring dip  /  Abduction ↔ QL raise ↔ L-sit  /  Neck
  Fri  RDL ↔ stretch  /  Split squat ↔ incline ↔ tib  /  Hyper ↔ HSPU ↔ calf  /  Nordic ↔ pulldown  /  Elephant walk

WITHIN a big day, the four heavy movements each get their OWN superset, so none
of them rests against another that taxes the same tissue. The one deliberate
pairing is NORDIC CURL with the PULL: Nordics tax neither grip nor low back, so
the pulldown or row rides their rest for free.

The fillers in a big day's supersets are chosen to tax NEITHER hamstrings NOR
the low back — the incline press, handstand push-ups, the tib bar. Grip is the
exception: these ARE the grip days, so the one-arm kettlebell calf raise belongs
here and nowhere else. It rests the hyper, which needs no grip of its own.

Low back lands Mon/Wed/Fri only, so it is never trained on consecutive days.

The three drivers
-----------------
Everything else is a maintenance dose. These three progress:

1. LOW BACK — hyperextension, one-legged CURVED-BACK on the 45-degree bench.
   Ramp REPS to 35 per side for 3 sets before any load goes on. Starting at
   5/side (logged as 10 total). 3 sets x 3 days = 9 direct sets a week.
   The flat bench at the machine gym is the HARDER version — peak load lands at
   full extension rather than partway down — so the one-legged work finished
   there transfers favourably to the 45 here. Estimated 11–35 weeks to the
   3 x 35/side target; the flat-bench carryover argues for the low end.

2. ADDUCTORS — side-lying hyperextension adductor raise, same reps-then-load
   structure. Currently 8 per side (logged as 16 total). These live on the LIGHT
   days: no grip, no hamstrings, no spinal extension, so they don't belong on the
   big days. 6 sets x 2 days = 12/wk, clearing the floor on its own.

3. FRONT RACK SPLIT SQUAT — barbell, +5 lb/week, 70 → 135 over 13 weeks.
   The 135 is not the goal in itself: it is how you earn a MAINTENANCE weight
   worth holding. Strength is kept by training at the same LOAD, not the same
   volume — drop the weight and the ability goes with it — so a heavier
   maintenance weight preserves more ability for fewer sets.
   The limiter is strength in the deep position, NOT the rack (185 on a
   conventional front squat settles that). So depth and pause time progress
   alongside load — see the Wednesday paused exposure below.

Volume
------
A muscle needs >=12 sets/week (secondaries at 0.5, pooled across every movement)
to be PROGRESSED. Below 12 is maintenance. Weekly totals from this plan:

  Progressing: Hamstrings 34, Back (Lower) 24.5, Gluteals 24.5, Quadriceps 18.5,
    Adductors 17.5, Calves 15.5, Tibialis 12.
  Maintenance: Latissimus Dorsi 9, Abductors 6, Quadratus Lumborum 6.

Those pooled numbers run high because the clustering concentrates everything
posterior onto three days. The numbers that matter for the progressions are the
DIRECT ones: 9 hyper sets, 12 Nordic sets, 11 RDL sets, 11 split squat sets,
12 side hyper sets a week.

Movement notes
--------------
- RDL: snatch-grip, NO straps (grip trained raw). HELD STEADY at 155 while the
  hypers progress. Ceiling for this gym is 300 — past that means buying plates,
  and 300 is well beyond what the back needs. Wednesday is the same lift at 135
  for 3 sets. Warm-up ramps are logged as warm-up sets, so they add session time
  but not volume. Leads every big day, on the freshest back.
- Couch stretch rides the RDL rest, done AT the bar so it's never abandoned, and
  warms the deep position the split squat is limited by. 2 min per side, logged
  as one set of 2 sides x 120 s.
- Front rack split squat: 12 reps/side = 24 total; weight is what's on the bar.
  Mon/Fri are the heavy ramp (4 sets at the week's weight), warming up with
  bodyweight then the empty bar. Add 5 lb a week.
- WEDNESDAY'S SPLIT SQUAT IS THE PAUSED ONE: lighter load, 3–5 s held at full
  depth, 8 reps/side. This is the end-range strength work — the deep position is
  the limiter, so progress pause time and depth here, not load. These sets count
  toward volume; they're real work, not a warm-up.
- Hyperextension: one-legged, curved back, reps logged as the TOTAL across both
  sides. Do NOT add load until 3 sets of 35/side is clean. Target load after that
  is 45 lb for 3 x 12/side, and hold there — progress reps, not weight. The
  loaded hyper's failure mode is the spine flexing when the erectors fade, and
  load raises the shear cost faster than reps do.
  If you want the long lever, hold the plate BEHIND THE HEAD IN YOUR HANDS (or
  arms overhead) — never resting on the head or neck. Same moment arm, no
  cervical load, and the hands fail before the spine does.
- NORDIC CURL is the hamstring TENDON slot, not a hypertrophy lift. A slow heavy
  eccentric is the high-strain, ~3-second loading tendons adapt to. It stays at
  4 sets: tendon adaptation saturates at roughly a minute of high-strain time
  per session, so more volume buys nothing and costs recovery.
  When you next do a LOADED curl (ankle strap on the low row), turn the toes OUT
  — that biases biceps femoris, whose distal tendon shares the fibular head with
  the LCL. That's the reason this slot exists. Cable Leg Curl is registered in
  exercises/ if you want to add it; it's left out here because it would stack
  more hamstring volume onto an already-clustered day.
  ("90% of max" in the tendon literature means 90% of a maximal ISOMETRIC
  contraction, not 90% of a 1RM. The dynamic equivalent is a 6RM moved slowly.)
- Side-lying hyperextension adductor raise: reps logged as the total across both
  sides. Ramp reps, then load the same way as the hyper.
- Cable hip abduction: MAINTENANCE, 3 sets x 2 days = 6/wk, ankle strap so it
  costs no grip. Keep the LOAD high — a light dose only maintains if the weight
  stays up. The machine-gym ad/ab ramp ends when that stint does; this holds
  what it built.
- Tibialis on the tib bar, one-legged (the harder version): 35/side = 70 total,
  4 sets x 3 days = 12/wk.
- The pull (pulldown Mon/Fri, low row Wednesday) is the first real pulling this
  program has had since jujitsu stopped supplying it. It only ever appears
  supersetted with the Nordic curl.
- Calf raise: 40 lb kettlebell in ONE hand, 35 reps per side, logged as 70 total.
  Grip work, so it lives on the big days — 3 sets x 3 days = 9/wk, maintenance.
- Ring dips and L-sits are support grip, not the crush grip the RDL, the pulls
  and the one-arm calf raise tax, so they're allowed on the light days.
- L-sit off the handstand bars: compression / hip flexors, 30 s holds.
- Elephant walk: single set, Friday finisher — posterior-chain decompression.

Neck (harness on the cable)
---------------------------
Front / back / sides, 20 reps @ 30, 4 sets each, done LAST on Tue/Thu.

PROGRESSION IS BY SETS, not load. Each direction is its own muscle — flexors,
extensors and lateral flexors don't cross-train — so each gets its own 12-set
weekly floor. That's 6 sets/session per direction (x2 days = 12/wk each). You're
at 4.

Normally the gate is DELAYED soreness landing on a jujitsu day. WITH NO JUJITSU
IN MONTH ONE THAT GATE IS GONE — so month one is when to ramp this fast: add a
set per direction per week and be at 6 by the time jujitsu resumes. Hold 20 reps
@ 30 through the whole volume ramp; load comes after, as double progression
(reps to ~35, then the smallest weight bump, reset reps).

Sides: one set = BOTH sides, logged as 40 total reps. The volume tool lumps all
neck into one "Neck" number, so at target it reads ~36 — that's 12 per real
muscle.

Equipment note: Chicago has no neck machine. The harness off the lat pulldown or
low row is the version this assumes — buy it before the stint starts, or the
neck ramp stalls.

Open items
----------
- Weights in the .fnw are starting targets — adjust in FitNotes as you go. The
  cable abduction (60), incline (135), pulldown and low row (120) are
  placeholders; no Chicago history existed to read them off.
- TIME is the risk on the big days: four separate supersets plus warm-ups is a
  lot to fit in 90 min. If one runs long, Wednesday is the day to trim — it is
  already the lighter version of the same shape.
- Jujitsu returns after month one at a frequency not yet decided. When it does,
  the neck ramp should be finished and the big days may need re-spacing.
- What comes AFTER this block: the low back drops to a maintenance dose at the
  same load, the split squat holds at 135, and the freed slots become reactive /
  frontal-plane work for the right knee (lateral bounds with a stuck landing,
  perturbation work) plus one heavy-slow calf slot for the Achilles. None of that
  starts until the back rehab is done.
