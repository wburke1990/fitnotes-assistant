CHI — progression notes
=======================

Companion to the CHI - <Day>.fnw plans (a .fnw has no notes field, so the cues
that don't fit as reps/weight live here). Generated by scripts/programs/chi_block.py.

This is the FIRST plan written for the Chicago home gym. Everything else under
plans/ is a travel gym: plans/wh/ is the machine gym, and plans/pit/ (with its
duplicate plans/pa/) plus plans/back_rehab/ and plans/back_rehab_jj/ are all
Pittsburgh.

Context
-------
Lifting Monday–Friday, up to ~90 min a session, no weekends. NO jujitsu for the
first month back. That makes month one the best lifting window of the block: no
grappling soreness, no recovery tax, and no delayed-soreness gate on the neck.

The two constraints that shape the week
---------------------------------------
Both point the same way, which is why the week splits into three big days and
two light ones.

1. GRIP is a long-standing limiter. Heavy grip on back-to-back days does not
   recover. So every grip-heavy movement is CLUSTERED onto the same days: the
   snatch-grip RDL (trained raw, no straps), the pulldown, the low row, and any
   loaded carry. The light days carry NO grip work: ring dips are support grip
   rather than crush grip, and the calf raise — a 40 lb kettlebell in one hand —
   is light enough that it doesn't tire the hands.

2. The POSTERIOR CHAIN is the same story. RDLs, hamstring work and the hypers
   cannot be spread across alternating days and recovered. So they cluster too,
   and the front rack split squat joins them — it taxes the hamstrings hard even
   though it costs no grip.

Wednesday runs the SAME SHAPE as Monday and Friday at reduced VOLUME, rather
than dropping movements. Alternating hinge days with non-hinge days is the
pattern that doesn't recover; a smaller day of the same shape does.

Note what Wednesday cuts: the RDL keeps its 155 and loses two sets. It does NOT
drop to a lighter bar. The RDL is a maintenance lift here — held steady while
the hypers drive — and strength is maintained by training at the same LOAD, not
the same volume. A lighter Wednesday bar would be neither heavy enough to hold
anything nor light enough to be free, which is the definition of junk volume.
Filling the day with high-rep RDL instead is off the table: form degrades at
high reps, and it degrades exactly where the back is being rehabbed. The only
movement that goes lighter on Wednesday is the split squat, and only because
Wednesday runs the paused version.

Schedule
--------
(↔ = superset.)

  Mon  Stretch ↔ RDL  /  Nordic ↔ pulldown ↔ split-squat ramp  /  Split squat ↔ low row  /  Hypers
  Tue  Side hyper ↔ ring dip ↔ calf  /  Incline ↔ HSPU ↔ tib ↔ L-sit  /  Abduction ↔ QL raise ↔ face pull  /  Wrist ext ↔ wrist rot  /  Neck
  Wed  Stretch ↔ RDL (2 sets)  /  Nordic ↔ pulldown ↔ split-squat ramp  /  Paused split squat ↔ low row  /  Hypers
  Thu  Side hyper ↔ ring dip ↔ calf  /  Incline ↔ HSPU ↔ tib ↔ L-sit  /  Abduction ↔ QL raise ↔ face pull  /  Wrist ext ↔ wrist rot  /  Neck
  Fri  Stretch ↔ RDL  /  Nordic ↔ pulldown ↔ split-squat ramp  /  Split squat ↔ low row  /  Hypers  /  Elephant walk

A BIG DAY IS FOUR BLOCKS AND NOTHING ELSE: hinge, the Nordic block, the working
split squat, then hypers to finish. Each heavy movement gets its own superset so
none of them rests against another taxing the same tissue. Everything that could
be done on a light day was moved to one, because the big days are the ones short
on time — that's where the pressing and the tib bar went.

Everything else rides a rest. The VERTICAL PULL rides the Nordic's, because
Nordics tax neither grip nor low back. The SPLIT-SQUAT RAMP rides it too, one
rung per round — bodyweight, bodyweight, empty bar — so the ramp costs no
separate time and you arrive at the bar warm. The HORIZONTAL PULL rides the
working split squat's rest, which the front rack can afford because it costs no
grip.

THE STRETCH LEADS THE HINGE BLOCK rather than trailing it, and that ordering is
load-bearing. FitNotes renders an exercise's warm-up sets at the top of its
entry, so a stretch listed second would land BEFORE the RDL's ramp instead of
inside it. Listed first, the round-robin runs stretch → RDL → stretch → RDL,
which puts a 2-minute bout between the 135 warm-up and the first 155 working
set, and between every working set after that.

Low back lands Mon/Wed/Fri only, so it is never trained on consecutive days.

The three drivers
-----------------
Everything else is a maintenance dose. These three progress:

1. LOW BACK — hyperextension on the 45-degree bench, 3 sets x 3 days = 9 direct
   sets a week, finishing every big day on its own.
   THE SET IS NEVER SHORTENED. Every set is at least 35 reps: open with as many
   reps as you currently own of the hardest variant (one-legged curved-back,
   ~10 now), then finish the set as regular reps. What progresses is the HARD
   FRACTION growing, not the set getting shorter — so the logged rep count only
   ever goes up. At 35+ reps a set is long enough to need no filler to rest
   against, which is why the block has nothing else in it.
   The ladder's top rung is all 35 per side done as the hard variant (logged as
   70). Only then does load go on.
   The flat bench at the machine gym is the HARDER version — peak load lands at
   full extension rather than partway down — so the one-legged work finished
   there transfers favourably to the 45 here. Estimated 11–35 weeks to the
   3 x 35/side target; the flat-bench carryover argues for the low end.

2. ADDUCTORS — side-lying hyperextension adductor raise, same reps-then-load
   structure. Currently 8 per side (logged as 16 total). These live on the LIGHT
   days: no grip, no hamstrings, no spinal extension, so they don't belong on the
   big days. 6 sets x 2 days = 12/wk, clearing the floor on its own.

3. FRONT RACK SPLIT SQUAT — barbell, +5 lb/week, 70 → 135 over 13 weeks.
   The 135 is not the goal in itself: it is how you earn a MAINTENANCE weight
   worth holding. Strength is kept by training at the same LOAD, not the same
   volume — drop the weight and the ability goes with it — so a heavier
   maintenance weight preserves more ability for fewer sets.
   The limiter is strength in the deep position, NOT the rack (185 on a
   conventional front squat settles that). So depth and pause time progress
   alongside load — see the Wednesday paused exposure below.

Volume
------
A muscle needs >=12 sets/week (secondaries at 0.5, pooled across every movement)
to be PROGRESSED. Below 12 is maintenance. Weekly totals from this plan:

  Progressing: Hamstrings 38.5, Back (Lower) 32.5, Gluteals 29.5, Quadriceps
    26.5, Adductors 20, Latissimus Dorsi 18, Trapezius 15, Calves 12.5,
    Tibialis 12.
  Maintenance: Biceps 9, Abductors 6, Quadratus Lumborum 6.

Those pooled numbers run high because the clustering concentrates everything
posterior onto three days. The numbers that matter for the progressions are the
DIRECT ones: 9 hyper sets, 12 Nordic sets, 10 RDL sets, 11 working split squat
sets (plus 8 on the ramp), 12 side hyper sets a week.

Movement notes
--------------
- RDL: snatch-grip, NO straps (grip trained raw). HELD STEADY at 155 while the
  hypers progress. Ceiling for this gym is 300 — past that means buying plates,
  and 300 is well beyond what the back needs. Wednesday is the same lift at the
  same 155, two sets instead of four. Its warm-up ramp is logged as warm-up sets,
  so it adds session time but not volume. Opens every big day, on the freshest
  back — though the stretch is listed ahead of it in the block, for the reason
  above.
- Couch stretch: FOUR bouts, ONE SIDE each, 2 min per bout, alternating
  L/R/L/R across the four rounds — so both sides get two bouts and every RDL
  working set is preceded by a stretch. Done AT the bar so it's never abandoned.
  It opens the deep position the split squat is limited by, three blocks later.
  Logged as 1 side x 120 s per set.
- Front rack split squat: 12 reps/side = 24 total; weight is what's on the bar.
  Mon/Fri are 4 working sets at the week's weight. Add 5 lb a week.
  THE RAMP IS NOT A WARM-UP BLOCK. It appears as a second split-squat entry
  inside the Nordic block — bodyweight, bodyweight, empty bar, one per round —
  so it uses rest that was happening anyway. Those reps count; they're real.
  Two bodyweight rungs, not one: the deep position needs more opening than the
  load does, and inside someone else's rest the reps are free.
- WEDNESDAY'S SPLIT SQUAT IS THE PAUSED ONE: lighter load, 3–5 s held at full
  depth, 8 reps/side, with two bodyweight rungs in the Nordic block before it.
  This is the end-range strength work — the deep position is the limiter, so
  progress pause time and depth here, not load.
- Hyperextension: see driver 1 above for the rep-transformation rule. Reps are
  logged as the TOTAL across both sides. Do NOT add load until 3 sets of 35/side
  is clean. Target load after that is 45 lb for 3 x 12/side, and hold there —
  progress reps, not weight. The loaded hyper's failure mode is the spine flexing
  when the erectors fade, and load raises the shear cost faster than reps do.
  If you want the long lever, hold the plate BEHIND THE HEAD IN YOUR HANDS (or
  arms overhead) — never resting on the head or neck. Same moment arm, no
  cervical load, and the hands fail before the spine does.
- NORDIC CURL is the hamstring TENDON slot, not a hypertrophy lift. A slow heavy
  eccentric is the high-strain, ~3-second loading tendons adapt to. It stays at
  4 sets: tendon adaptation saturates at roughly a minute of high-strain time
  per session, so more volume buys nothing and costs recovery.
  When you next do a LOADED curl (ankle strap on the low row), turn the toes OUT
  — that biases biceps femoris, whose distal tendon shares the fibular head with
  the LCL. That's the reason this slot exists. Cable Leg Curl is registered in
  exercises/ if you want to add it; it's left out here because it would stack
  more hamstring volume onto an already-clustered day.
  ("90% of max" in the tendon literature means 90% of a maximal ISOMETRIC
  contraction, not 90% of a 1RM. The dynamic equivalent is a 6RM moved slowly.)
- Side-lying hyperextension adductor raise: reps logged as the total across both
  sides. Ramp reps, then load the same way as the hyper.
- Cable hip abduction: MAINTENANCE, 3 sets x 2 days = 6/wk, ankle strap so it
  costs no grip. Keep the LOAD high — a light dose only maintains if the weight
  stays up. The machine-gym ad/ab ramp ends when that stint does; this holds
  what it built.
- Tibialis on the tib bar, one-legged (the harder version): 35/side = 70 total.
  Moved off the big days to save time there; 6 sets x 2 days still clears its
  12/wk floor, just on two exposures instead of three.
- The pull is the first real pulling this program has had since jujitsu stopped
  supplying it. Every big day gets both angles: lat pulldown on the Nordic's
  rest, low row on the split squat's. 9 sets each, 18/wk.
- Calf raise: 40 lb kettlebell in ONE hand, 35 reps per side, logged as 70 total.
  A one-arm hold, but 40 lb doesn't tire the hands, so it's exempt from the grip
  cluster and sits on the light days — 3 sets x 2 days = 6/wk, maintenance. That
  also keeps three sets off the big days, which are the ones short on time.
- Ring dips are support grip, not the crush grip the RDL and the pulls tax, so
  they're allowed on the light days.
- WRIST PREHAB (extension + rotation) is on the light days, and that is not a
  violation of the grip cluster: the extensors and rotators are the ANTAGONISTS
  to crush grip, not more of it, and the loads are small. 15 reps/side = 30
  total, 4 sets each x 2 days = 8/wk, run back to back as an antagonist pair.
  At the machine gym these ran 6 sets each because the air machine could not add
  load, so volume was the only lever left. Here they load, so they progress on
  WEIGHT at half the sets.
  Extension is a dumbbell reverse wrist curl — the extensors are weak enough
  that 25 lb is plenty. ROTATION WANTS A BARBELL held at one end: the long lever
  beats a 25 lb dumbbell outright, and you adjust the resistance by choking up
  or down the shaft. That matters because rotation is the one that was near the
  air machine's ceiling.

Upper body
----------
ALL THE PUSHING IS ON TUE/THU — incline, handstand push-ups, ring dips. Pressing
costs neither grip nor hamstrings, so it has no business taking up time on a big
day, and the light days have room for it. That is the pushing day.

PULLING is 18 sets a week against 24 of pressing — close enough to balanced.
Grip confines it to the big days, but there are two rests there that can carry
it: the Nordic's (pulldown) and the working split squat's (low row). Both are
free — neither host movement needs grip.

FACE PULLS are the exception that earns a light-day slot: 3 sets x 2 days = 6/wk.
They're the only external-rotation / rear-delt work in the plan — the antagonist
to every grappling pull and post — and light enough that the rope costs no
meaningful grip. They matter most from month two, when jujitsu comes back.

Handstand push-up ramp: currently around 80 degrees off the parallettes. Incline
was pushed hard last year and starts at 135; adjust from your own history.
- L-sit off the handstand bars: compression / hip flexors, 30 s holds.
- Elephant walk: single set, Friday finisher — posterior-chain decompression.

Neck (harness on the cable)
---------------------------
Front / back / sides, 20 reps @ 30, 4 sets each, done LAST on Tue/Thu.

PROGRESSION IS BY SETS, not load. Each direction is its own muscle — flexors,
extensors and lateral flexors don't cross-train — so each gets its own 12-set
weekly floor. That's 6 sets/session per direction (x2 days = 12/wk each). You're
at 4.

Normally the gate is DELAYED soreness landing on a jujitsu day. WITH NO JUJITSU
IN MONTH ONE THAT GATE IS GONE — so month one is when to ramp this fast: add a
set per direction per week and be at 6 by the time jujitsu resumes. Hold 20 reps
@ 30 through the whole volume ramp; load comes after, as double progression
(reps to ~35, then the smallest weight bump, reset reps).

Sides: one set = BOTH sides, logged as 40 total reps. The volume tool lumps all
neck into one "Neck" number, so at target it reads ~36 — that's 12 per real
muscle.

Equipment note: Chicago has no neck machine. The harness off the lat pulldown or
low row is the version this assumes — buy it before the stint starts, or the
neck ramp stalls.

Open items
----------
- Weights in the .fnw are starting targets — adjust in FitNotes as you go. The
  cable abduction (60), incline (135), pulldown and low row (120) are
  placeholders; no Chicago history existed to read them off.
- TIME: the big days are now four lean blocks and should come in well under
  90 min. The pressure has moved to TUE/THU, which absorbed the pushing and the
  tib bar and are no longer short days — they run roughly 40 sets. If those go
  long, the neck block is at the end and can be cut mid-session without
  disturbing anything else.
- The hyper block grows through the meso. Three sets of 35 is quick now; three
  sets of 70 is not. Expect the big days to lengthen as driver 1 progresses.
- Jujitsu returns after month one at a frequency not yet decided. When it does,
  the neck ramp should be finished and the big days may need re-spacing.
- What comes AFTER this block: the low back drops to a maintenance dose at the
  same load, the split squat holds at 135, and the freed slots become reactive /
  frontal-plane work for the right knee (lateral bounds with a stuck landing,
  perturbation work) plus one heavy-slow calf slot for the Achilles. None of that
  starts until the back rehab is done.
